# Stage 4 usage: give the adapted model its chat behavior back

Stage 3 made Urdu cheaper and better on likelihood, but training a chat model on plain Wikipedia text broke its chat behavior: with thinking on (Qwen3's default) it usually ended its turn after the reasoning, with no answer. Stage 4 repairs that with a short supervised fine-tuning (SFT) step on Urdu and English conversations written by a larger Qwen3, and measures the result against the base model and the Stage 3 model.

**Read this first.** The Stage 4 model answers with thinking on and off, replies to Urdu in Urdu, and keeps the Stage 3 gains. Its Urdu answers are better than the base Qwen3-1.7B's but not reliable: long answers can fall into repetition and facts are often wrong. With thinking off it answers reasoning questions too quickly and gets more of them wrong. The Urdu was judged by Claude, not by a native speaker. The whole pipeline is tested on Qwen3 only.

## What Stage 4 did, and how

**The idea.** Fine-tune the Stage 3 model on conversations in exactly the format it will be served in (Qwen3's chat template, with and without thinking), with the loss only on the assistant's reply. A model that sees thousands of replies that close their reasoning and then answer learns to do that again.

**The data, written by a larger open model.** No Urdu dataset has reasoning traces, so the conversations were generated by **Qwen3-14B-AWQ** (Apache 2.0), served with vLLM on Kaggle's two T4s. It was picked over Qwen3-8B in a side-by-side trial (notebook 12): similar Urdu, half as many loops. Commercial API models were not used to write training data.

- **Grounded in Urdu Wikipedia.** Each conversation starts from a passage of the Stage 3 training split, checked against FLORES and Belebele. In *grounded* conversations the user gives the passage and asks about it (summarize, explain, list, a factual question). In *standalone* ones the user asks a question that makes sense on its own and the teacher sees the passage only as hidden background. Standalone questions were used only with thinking off, because with thinking on the teacher's reasoning kept citing the hidden background.
- **The mix:** 10,000 conversations planned, 80% Urdu and 20% English, about half with thinking. 11,502 were generated (notebook 13, two shards of about 3.6 hours) and 9,854 kept after automatic filters: failed request, reply cut off, reasoning block in the wrong mode or not closed, wrong language, words that mix Urdu and Latin letters (a decoding glitch of the 4-bit teacher), repetition loops, mentions of the hidden source, duplicates, and near copies of the evaluation prompts.
- **Read by a reader.** A sample of 150 kept conversations was read and marked: 71% pass (English 90%, Urdu 66%). The Urdu fails are mostly wrong or invented words, misread passages and wrong facts that no automatic filter can see. This review was done by Claude, not a native Urdu speaker. Training went ahead with this data, and its quality is the main limitation of the result.

**Training (notebook 14).** LoRA of rank 64 on all attention and MLP projections of the merged Stage 3 model, fp16 on one T4, learning rate 1e-4, 2 epochs (140 steps of 16 rows of 4,096 tokens), the new Urdu embedding rows frozen. Conversations were packed into rows of 4,096 tokens with attention kept inside each conversation; a check before step 1 compares a conversation's logits alone and packed. Testing that check found a real trap: Transformers 5.22 keeps packed sequences apart only when no generation cache is passed, so every training call passes `use_cache=False`. 658 tokens per second, 12.4 GB, about 4 hours. The held-out loss per reply token fell from 1.060 (step 20) to 0.970 (step 140). LoRA was merged in float32 and the result checked against the unmerged model.

**Evaluation (notebook 15).** The base, Stage 3 and Stage 4 models were served by vLLM (float16) and given the same 60 hand-written prompts (40 Urdu, 20 English; written for this check and never shown to the teacher), greedy, thinking on and off. Quality was re-measured with the Stage 3 protocol; the Stage 3 model reproduced its earlier numbers.

## What a reader sees

All 240 base and Stage 4 replies were read side by side (by Claude; details in `docs/decisions.md`, 2026-11-28).

- **Urdu: clearly better than the base.** Under greedy decoding the base Qwen3-1.7B turns most Urdu explanations, stories and advice into repetition or nonsense. Stage 4 mostly writes readable, on-topic Urdu, and short facts and sums are right (water boils at 100 degrees, 8 pens at 25 rupees cost 200, "علم طاقت ہے").
- **But not reliable.** Long answers (a recipe, a leave application, a story) still slide into repetition. Facts are often wrong: the capital of Punjab given as Islamabad, the Atlantic as the largest ocean, Iqbal born in Multan, invented book titles.
- **English: close to the base with thinking on, worse at reasoning with thinking off.** With thinking off, Stage 4 answers in one line without working and got 1 of 4 English reasoning prompts right (base 4 of 4). With thinking on it got 4 of 4. The training answers without thinking were short and direct, and the model copied that.
- **The reasoning is in English**, also for Urdu questions, as the teacher's was. The answer that follows is in Urdu.

Use thinking on for anything that needs a calculation or a chain of steps.

## The commands at a glance

| Command | What it does | Needs a GPU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `zubaan tune check` | The chat-behavior check on any OpenAI-compatible server, or on a GGUF through llama-server: answer rate per thinking mode, reply language, loops, token-limit hits; every reply written to Markdown for reading | No (a server does the work) |
| `zubaan tune filter` | Filter and balance the teacher's answers, write the training file and a sample to read | No |
| `zubaan tune run` | LoRA SFT with the loss on the reply only, packing check, Hub checkpoints, resume | Yes |
| `zubaan tune merge` | Merge the LoRA into the Stage 3 model in float32, check the logits and the tokenizer, save | No, but the float32 merge does not fit in 8 GB RAM (notebook 14 runs it) |
| `zubaan export all` | GGUF with the parity check, Q4_K_M and Q8_0, Modelfile, vLLM config (as in Stage 3) | No |

Configs: `configs/chat-check.yaml`, `configs/tune-filter.yaml`, `configs/tune-qwen3-1.7b.yaml`. The chat-check prompt set is packaged as `src/zubaan/tune/prompts/chat_check_v1.yaml`. Install the training extras with `uv sync --group dev --extra audit --extra train`.

## Serving the Stage 4 model

Thinking can now stay on, which is Qwen3's default in vLLM and Ollama. The reasoning is in English; the answer follows in the language of the question.

**vLLM** (0.34.0, T4, `dtype half`) served the merged model in notebook 15 with the chat template from its folder. Thinking on is the default; to switch it off per request:

```json
{
  "model": "qwen3-urdu-chat",
  "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "پاکستان کا دارالحکومت کیا ہے؟"}],
  "chat_template_kwargs": {"enable_thinking": false}
}
```

**llama.cpp and Ollama.** Pending notebook 16: tokenizer parity, the Q4_K_M and Q8_0 files, their quantization cost, and the chat check on the Q4_K_M file. Normalize Urdu to NFC before sending it to a GGUF model (`zubaan.text.normalize_urdu` does), as in Stage 3.

## How to read the results

- **The base model is a weak bar for Urdu here.** Greedy decoding through vLLM makes the base Qwen3-1.7B loop on most open-ended Urdu prompts, so "no more token-limit hits than the base" is easy to pass. The thinking-on answer rate and the reading of the replies say more.
- **"Answered" means the model closed its reasoning and wrote an answer, not that the answer is right.** A fluent wrong answer counts as answered.
- **Bits per character moved a little.** FLORES Urdu rose 0.017 and held-out Urdu 0.048 bits per character after SFT. Both stay far below the base. Chat tuning trades a little plain-text likelihood for answering.
- **Belebele did not move** in a way that can be told apart from noise, in either language.

## Limitations

- **The Urdu was judged by Claude, not by a native speaker,** both the training sample and the evaluation replies. The prompt set was approved by the author from its English glosses.
- **The training data is about two thirds clean in Urdu.** The 4-bit 14B teacher writes fluent-looking Urdu with wrong words, wrong honorifics and wrong facts in about one answer in three, and the student can learn them. A later round should add pattern filters (the Prophet's honorific after other names, letters of other scripts inside Urdu words) and a stronger Urdu teacher or a second checking pass.
- **Thinking-off reasoning got worse** than the base's in English (1 of 4 against 4 of 4 on a small set). Fix in a later data round with worked answers without thinking.
- **Single-turn data only.** The four two-turn prompts of the check were answered, but multi-turn behavior was not trained.
- **One run, one seed, 60 evaluation prompts, greedy decoding.** The chat numbers are behavior counts on a small hand-written set, not a benchmark.
- **Safety behavior was not measured.** Qwen's own preference and safety tuning is not reproduced.
- **Tested on Qwen3 only.** The tools assume a byte-level BPE tokenizer, tied embedding and head, and a ChatML-style chat template with Qwen3's thinking switch. Other model families need work (`docs/roadmap.md`).
